How the model works

This is a demonstration build for the CS2C AI model. The geometry scoring and SV tasking shown here are simple stand-in rules, not the model's output.

Sensor architecture follows the public outline of the Space Force missile warning/tracking plan. SDA Tracking Layer (LEO, 1,000 km): Tranche 1 has 35 SVs in 5 planes; Tranche 2 adds 54 SVs (48 wide field of view plus 6 fire-control); Tranche 3 adds 72 SVs (about half missile-defense fire-control), launching from FY2029. MEO: Resilient MW/MT Epoch 1 has 12 SVs in 2 planes; Epoch 2 adds 10. Next-Gen OPIR adds GEO and 2 polar SVs. Plane counts for Tranches 2 and 3, the 81° inclination, MEO altitude and the GEO slots are assumptions where no public figure exists.

Everything here is notional and geometric: circular Walker orbits, spherical Earth, simple ballistic and glide profiles. No real sensor performance is modeled.

An SV sees a target when the line of sight clears the Earth and the slant range is inside the layer's notional limit for that flight phase:

Shared fields of view (notional half-angles): LEO WFOV 62° (whole Earth disk), LEO fire-control 8°, MEO 26°, GEO 9°, polar 10°. The simulated feed tasks every threat at once: an SV keeps custody of several threats when they all fit inside its field of view and call for the same IR band, which frees other SVs. ×n on a label is how many threats that SV holds.

Best collection geometry is the highest score: 40% slant range, 25% elevation of the SV above the target's horizon, 35% remaining access time (dwell, capped at 5 min). The pick is held unless another SV beats it by 15%, to avoid needless handovers.

IR band tasking (notional). Each SV collects in one band at a time from the bands its layer carries. LEO WFOV: SWIR (default) or MWIR. LEO fire-control: MWIR (default) or LWIR. MEO, GEO, polar: SWIR (default) or MWIR. Band fit depends on flight phase and on whether the target sits against space (above the horizon) or Earth (below the horizon):

The simulated feed chooses band and geometry together; the baseline leaves every SV on its default band.

Custody pairs the best SV with the partner that maximizes sin(stereo angle) × quality. Stereo needs at least 15° between lines of sight. Fire-control (FC) SVs have longer notional reach against dim glide vehicles, standing in for the medium field of view sensors. One SV is mono custody (angles only, no range). No SV is a gap.
